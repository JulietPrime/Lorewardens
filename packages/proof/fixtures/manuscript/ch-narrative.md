Osric told the council he would never guard the eastern bridge again.

Gwyn did not protect the heir when the riot came.

Maren fed the horses while Osric strained the water. "The ford will hold until morning," she said. They ate in the blue hour before the sun.

She promised the ferryman her silver ring if he would wait past midnight.

No one knew why the beacon tower had gone dark that autumn.

The road climbed away from the river in long switchbacks, and the riders took it slowly. Twice they passed shrines older than the kingdom itself. Beyond the second shrine the trees thinned and the wind carried the smell of snow. Osric checked the packs at the next rise and said nothing about the missing rope. Maren rode with her collar up and her eyes on the ridge line. By late afternoon the light went copper over the western hills. They made camp below a shelf of stone, and the horses grazed the thin grass to the last blade. The night came on without a sound, and the watch fires burned low along the line.

Boots on gravel. A door gave way. Torches flared along the wall. Steel met steel on the stair. Someone shouted the name Osric. The line broke and ran.

The ferry carries three riders, as the old law requires, and Osric checked the tally twice.

The gate slammed, and the riders screamed past the wall. Then the yard was calm again, and the men settled by the brazier to wait for dawn.

Osric asked the ferryman about the winter fares and got a straight answer.

Maren stood at the window while the yard emptied below. Osric wondered whether the tide would turn before the bell.

Gwyn remembered the night the beacon went dark, and she said nothing. Osric knew the price of her silence and kept the ledger closed.

Suddenly the horn blew from the gate tower. Maren counted the riders on the road below. The yard went calm when the banner showed.

I climbed the north stair with my hand on the wall. My lamp made a small circle, and I kept to the middle. We had agreed that I would count the turns aloud. The chalk rode in my sleeve past the sixth turn. At the ninth stair the wall opened into a guardroom. A table stood where the garrison had left it. Dust held the shape of the boots that left a season before. The sea spoke against the rock, and the room waited.

The bell rang. Doors opened. Men ran. Rain came.
